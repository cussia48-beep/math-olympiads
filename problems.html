Number Theory

1. Divisibility

Prove that for every integer n, n³−n is divisible by 6.

Since n³−n=n(n−1)(n+1), it is the product of three consecutive integers. One is divisible by 3 and at least one is even, so the product is divisible by 6.
Combinatorics

2. Pigeonhole

Among 13 people, prove that at least two were born in the same month.

There are only 12 months (boxes) and 13 people (objects). By the pigeonhole principle, one month contains at least two birthdays.
Algebra

3. Inequality

For positive a,b, show that a/b + b/a ≥ 2.

By AM-GM, a/b + b/a ≥ 2√((a/b)(b/a)) = 2. Equality occurs when a=b.
